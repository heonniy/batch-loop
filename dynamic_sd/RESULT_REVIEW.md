# Dynamic-Depth Self-Speculative Decoding — Run 2026-11-11/12 Result Review

**Status:** EXECUTED on one host (8×H200), 23:12–04:30 KST. **Main question** (EXPERIMENT_PLAN): when native per-token recurrent exits are on, which fixed-depth assumptions of self-speculative decoding (SSD) break, measured against a **matched native dynamic-depth AR** target?

- E1 matrix: smoke n=32, held-out pilot n=100, held-out main n=200 for the headline cells.
- E2 conditional acceptance.
- E3 physical cost: eager B=1, an R-call microbenchmark, batched Ouro at B∈{1,8,16,32,64}.

## TL;DR (what breaks)

1. **The SSD speedup is set by the draft depth d, not by the exit depth.** Under native exits, dynamic AR's sequential depth falls to E[D]. SSD's sequential cost per token has a floor near d (the WFD admits one token per d ticks; DtV drafts serially at depth d). So the relative speedup shrinks monotonically with E[D]:
   - Ouro-1.4B, d=1, held-out main n=200: WFD/AR is **2.29× fixed → 1.63× (τ=0.4) → 1.20× (τ=0.1)**. DtV/AR is 1.90× → 1.39× → **1.03×**.
   - Huginn, d=4, main n=200: **2.24× fixed → 1.79× (ε=0.1) → 1.52× (ε=0.2)**. DtV is 2.05× → 1.54× → 1.35×.
   - SSD still gains some absolute throughput, but much less than AR. At matched quality (Ouro τ=0.7 ≈ fixed accuracy) exits give AR +14% and WFD **+1%**.
   - At shallow depth there is a crossover where SSD ≤ dynamic AR (Ouro τ=0.1 with d=2: DtV 0.96×).
2. **Acceptance stops being stationary (Ouro): a selection effect.**
   - Ouro's gate sends hard tokens deep. P(accept | D) is 1.00 / 0.87 / 0.65 for D = 2/3/4 at τ=0.4 (n=200, 51k verifications). At fixed depth the same model accepts 0.91–0.95.
   - With the same mean depth assigned without the gate (const/shuffle traces), acceptance is flat at 0.90–0.93.
   - Huginn's latent-convergence exit carries almost no such signal: P(accept | D) is flat at 0.88–0.93 over the bulk of D.
3. **Tokens with D_t ≤ d need no speculation.** 59% (WFD boundary) to 66% (DtV exact-in-draft) of Ouro τ=0.1 tokens commit with nothing to verify. The acceptance-rate metric then describes only the remaining minority.
4. **The draft-depth decision becomes depth-regime dependent (Huginn).** At fixed depth, d=4 and d=8 are within 3% of each other (2.31× vs 2.25×, pilot). Under live exits d=8 loses 22% (1.79× vs 1.40×, ε=0.1, pilot; same in smoke at ε=0.05). Tuning d on fixed depth is unsafe.
5. **The batch synchronization policy flips the sign of the effect.**
   - In a lockstep batch, a decode step waits for the deepest row (E[max_b D] = 3.78 at B=32, 3.87 at B=64 vs E[D] ≈ 1.95). So AR-live gains only 1.11–1.12× from exits, and DtV verify runs to full depth.
   - Neither AR nor SSD realizes the exit savings, so SSD's *relative* speedup suffers less than at B=1.
   - Any "dynamic depth hurts or helps SSD" claim must name the AR denominator's depth scheduling.
6. **Approximate (shared) KV breaks SSD losslessness.**
   - Exact FULL-KV dynamic SSD equals matched dynamic AR token-for-token in FP32 (8/8 prompts, both models, both schedulers).
   - With a last-computed shared KV (`kv_budget_s=1`), SSD reads the in-flight depth state of earlier tokens and the targets diverge: FP32 Huginn WFD 6/8 and DtV 3/8 exact.
   - The KV policy also changes the realized depth (Huginn ε=0.1: E[D] 16.3 → 10.2).
   - So SSD speedups measured under approximate KV compare different targets.

What did **not** break: commit order, multi-token chain verification and rollback under live exits (FP32-exact); gate-evaluation overhead (replay ≈ live); natural depth variance at B=1 (low-variance traces change results by ≤ 4%); Ouro's best d (=1) and best K (=4).

## 3. What breaks — details

### 3.1 The speedup tracks E[D]/d
- AR-live's R/tok equals E[D] (Ouro τ=0.1: 1.71).
- WFD's R/tok stays near its floor: 1.18–1.27 for Ouro d=1, against 1.27 at fixed depth. For Huginn d=4 it moves only from 7.51 (fixed, rejection-driven) to 5.2 (ε=0.1) and 4.7 (ε=0.2).
- DtV's R/tok has the same kind of floor: serial drafting costs about d per drafted token, plus the verify depth per round (Ouro τ=0.1: 1.59).
- So the attainable sequential-step ratio is bounded by about E[D]/d. The measured wall speedup falls monotonically with E[D] in both models, with tight CIs (held-out table).
- The absolute gain from exits is small for SSD: Ouro WFD 45.8 → 54.8 tok/s at τ=0.1 (+20%) against AR +129%; Huginn d=4 WFD +37% against AR +77% at ε=0.1 (pilot: 29.6 → 40.6 vs 12.8 → 22.7).
- **Most of the relative loss is the denominator getting faster, but not all of it**: SSD's own exit benefit is capped by d.
- At matched quality (Ouro τ=0.7, pilot accuracy 0.75 = fixed 0.75) the gains are AR +14% and WFD +1% (24.3 vs 21.4; 49.3 vs 48.9 tok/s).
- Temperature 0.7 shows the same pattern: WFD/AR is 1.99× fixed and 1.52× at τ=0.4.
- Replay with the recorded trace reproduces the live numbers (Ouro τ=0.4: WFD 1.67× vs 1.62×; Huginn ε=0.1: 1.77× vs 1.80×). The collapse is depth geometry, not gate cost.

### 3.2 D_t ≤ d: the target is already available at draft depth
- **Ouro τ=0.1, d=1, main:**
  - 59% of committed tokens take WFD's boundary path: the verifier halted at depth ≤ d, so no draft exists to check.
  - The WFD wave narrows from a mean width of 3.6 to 1.7.
  - DtV commits 66% of tokens as exact-in-draft.
- **Ouro τ=0.4:** no token exits at depth 1, so this case vanishes.
- **Consequence:** the fixed-depth SSD notion of "acceptance rate α over all positions" no longer describes the workload. Only about 40% of tokens are speculative at all.

### 3.3 Depth-conditional acceptance
- **Ouro, main n=200, WFD verifier depth:**
  - τ=0.4: D2 **1.00** (n=27,785), D3 0.87, D4 **0.65**.
  - τ=0.1: D2 0.92, D3 0.76, D4 0.58.
- **Fixed depth:** D4 0.91–0.95.
- **Gate-free controls with the same mean depth** (τ=0.4 trace): `const` gives 0.93 / 0.92 and `shuffle` gives 0.92 / 0.91 / 0.90.
- DtV-live gives the same table, an independent check.
- **Interpretation:** the gate selects difficulty. Overall α is unchanged (0.92 live vs 0.92 const), but it is no longer exchangeable across tokens. A depth-aware speculation policy has real signal on Ouro.
- **Huginn, main n=200, ε=0.1:** 0.88–0.93 for D = 13…18, which holds 91% of the tokens; it dips to 0.80–0.83 only for D = 19…22. At ε=0.2 it drops to about 0.67 only in the rare deep tail. **Little signal on Huginn.**

- No correctness failure from native exits under FULL KV (FP32-exact).
- Gate overhead and live-vs-replay geometry differences: ≤ 3%.
- Speculative tokens exiting at a different depth than the accepted token: no measurable effect.
- Natural depth variance at B=1: ≤ 4%.
- Hindsight early-terminated verify: ≤ 8% of verify ticks.
- Ouro d and K: no decision change.

- With a single shared slot, a WFD/DtV token reads the **current (in-flight) depth** of earlier tokens, whereas AR reads their final depth.
- So **an approximate KV policy is no longer scheduling-invariant**: SSD and AR compute different targets even in FP32.
- Speedups under approximate KV must not be reported as lossless SSD speedups.
- The policy also shifts the exit-depth distribution (Huginn E[D] 16.3 → 10.2), which confounds KV savings with compute savings.
- The plan's Ouro protected-first-recurrence + last policy was **not** run; `s=1` is the LAST-only negative control.

## 6. Implications for a decision policy (E6 baselines only, no new method claimed)
1. **Pick d by the realized depth regime** (Huginn): d=4 for live exits, either d for fixed depth. This is the one decision shift measured (22%).
2. **Fall back to AR-live when E[D] ≲ 1.5·d.** This covers the Ouro τ=0.1 d=2 crossover (DtV 0.96×), but there is no gain when d=1 is available.
3. **Depth-aware speculation for Ouro.** Signal exists (D4 accept 0.58–0.65), but rejected work is nearly free at B=1 in this engine. Its value would only appear in a row-bound engine. Not demonstrated.
4. **Batched serving:** exit savings, and hence any SSD-vs-dynamic-AR trade-off, depend on depth-asynchronous scheduling. Lockstep loses them for both AR and SSD.
5. **Early-terminated verify:** ≤ 8% bound. Not worth pursuing at B=1.

## 7. Caveats and limits
- **Eager, launch-bound harness.** With CUDA graphs the per-call base cost shrinks, so per-row work, rejected rows and wave width weigh more. Expect the E[D]/d mechanism to hold, but not the exact ratios.
- **Sample sizes.** Smoke n=32 (one pool, also used for calibration) covers most of the matrix. Pilot n=100 and main n=200 are held out but cover only headline cells. Timing repeats are across prompts, with no separate repeated runs, except the batch's 2 cohorts at B ∈ {1, 8, 32}.
- **Neighbour load.** From 02:16 onward, burners restarted on finished GPUs while some jobs (Huginn main-fixed, pilot MATH, KV arms) were still running. Since arms are interleaved per prompt, ratios are robust; absolute tok/s for those late cells may be slightly low.
- **One contaminated cell.** A 2–3 minute functional test shared GPU 3 during smoke `huginn_math_fixed_d4`.
- **Batched code** is Ouro-only and lockstep (no WFD-batch arm, no unmasked/padded arm), uses equal cropped prompts, and has no EOS.
- **Not run:** Ouro protected-bank KV arms, LoopSpec (SGLang) reproduction, vllm-rlt, BBH/code tasks, and refill/continuous batching.
- **loopserving.code** (`casys-kaist-internal/loopserving.code@smcho/decision-placement`) was inspected read-only via `gh api` GET. No content from it is included in this repository.
